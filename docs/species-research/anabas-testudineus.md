## 3. Identification

| Aspect | Details | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Key Features** | Deep, laterally compressed body; large, protrusible mouth; ctenoid scales; single long dorsal fin with strong spines (16-18 spines, 9-11 soft rays); anal fin with 9-11 spines, 8-10 soft rays; pelvic fins thoracic, with 1 spine + 5 soft rays; caudal fin rounded; accessory air-breathing organ (suprabranchial chamber); coloration olive-green to brown with dark vertical bars; juveniles with ocellus on caudal peduncle | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Diagnostic Characteristics** | Body depth 2.0-2.5 x SL; head length 3.0-3.5 x SL; mouth terminal, protrusible; eyes large; scales ctenoid, 30-35 lateral line scales; dorsal fin origin above pectoral base; 16-18 dorsal spines, 9-11 soft rays; 9-11 anal spines, 8-10 soft rays; 11-13 pectoral rays; 1+5 pelvic rays; vertebrae 26-28 | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Distinguishing Features** | Only genus in Anabantidae with *Anabas*; distinguished from *Ctenopoma* (African) by distribution and fewer dorsal spines; unique ability to "walk" on land using pectoral fins and opercular spines; suprabranchial organ highly developed | FishBase, FAO, Britz et al. 2016 |
| **Similar Species** | *Anabas cobojius* (Gangetic Climbing Perch) — similar, possible synonym; *Ctenopoma* spp. (African climbing perches) — different distribution; *Microctenopoma* spp. — smaller, African | FishBase, FAO, Britz et al. 2016 |

**Taxonomic Uncertainty**: *Anabas cobojius* (Hamilton, 1822) from Ganges system may be a valid species or synonym of *A. testudineus*. FishBase treats as synonym; some regional works treat as valid. Requires review.

## 4. Biology

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Physical Description** | Deep, laterally compressed body; large head; large protrusible mouth; ctenoid scales; single dorsal fin with strong spines (16-18) and soft rays (9-11); anal fin with 9-11 spines, 8-10 soft rays; caudal fin rounded; suprabranchial air-breathing organ; coloration olive-green to brown dorsally with 8-10 dark vertical bars; ventral pale; juveniles with dark ocellus on caudal peduncle | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Size** | Max TL: 25 cm (rarely 30 cm); common: 10-20 cm TL; size at maturity: 8-12 cm TL | FishBase, FAO |
| **Weight** | Max: 200-300 g; common: 50-150 g | FishBase, FAO |
| **Lifespan** | Estimated 3-5 years in wild; up to 8 years in captivity; no validated age studies | FishBase (no authoritative source) |
| **Anatomy/Morphology** | Suprabranchial chamber (labyrinth organ) highly developed for air breathing; gill rakers short; ctenoid scales; opercular spines used for terrestrial locomotion; vertebrae 26-28; suprabranchial organ with complex folded epithelium | FishBase, FAO, Graham 1997 |
| **Physiology** | Obligate air-breather (must surface every few hours); can survive out of water for 6-10 hours if moist; "walks" on land using pectoral fins and opercular spines; tolerates very low DO; temperature 15-35°C | FishBase, FAO, Graham 1997 |
| **Behaviour** | Nocturnal/crepuscular; solitary; territorial; capable of overland movement between water bodies (hence "walking fish"); burrows in mud during drought; aestivates in dry season | FishBase, FAO |
| **Communication** | Sound production via gas bladder; likely chemical cues | FishBase |
| **Reproduction** | Oviparous; year-round with peaks in monsoon (Jun-Sep); floating bubble nest built by male among vegetation; adhesive eggs; male guards nest and fans eggs; fecundity: 500-5,000 eggs per spawn; eggs hatch 24-30 hrs; male guards fry for 1-2 weeks | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Life Cycle** | Rapid growth; sexual maturity at 6-9 months; multiple spawns per year; juveniles in marginal vegetation; parental care (male guards nest/fry) | FishBase, FAO |
