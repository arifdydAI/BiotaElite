## 1. Channa punctata — Spotted Snakehead / Gachua

### Identity
- **Scientific Name:** Channa punctata (Bloch, 1793)
- **Common Name (EN):** Spotted Snakehead / Gachua
- **Bengali Name:** গাছুয়া / শোল
- **Authority:** Bloch, 1793 (originally *Ophiocephalus punctatus*)

### Taxonomy
| Rank | Name | ID | Status |
|------|------|-----|--------|
| Kingdom | Animalia | animalia | ✓ |
| Phylum | Chordata | chordata | ✓ |
| Class | Actinopterygii | actinopterygii | ✓ |
| Order | Anabantiformes | anabantiformes | ✓ |
| Family | Channidae | channidae | ✓ |
| Genus | Channa | channa | ✓ |
| Species | Channa punctata | — | Need species node |

### Key Features (FishBase, IUCN, FAO)
- Elongated, cylindrical body; depressed head; large mouth with villiform teeth
- Single long dorsal fin (31-36 rays); long anal fin (22-26 rays)
- Caudal fin rounded; pelvic fins absent
- Body brownish with numerous dark spots; ventral surface pale
- Juveniles with distinct ocellus on caudal peduncle

### Diagnostic Characteristics (Talwar & Jhingran 1991, FishBase)
- Body depth 5.5-6.5 in SL; head length 2.8-3.3 in SL
- Eyes dorsolateral; mouth large, terminal; teeth villiform in bands
- Dorsal fin 31-36 rays; anal fin 22-26 rays
- Scales cycloid, 50-60 lateral line scales

### Distinguishing Features (FishBase, FAO)
- Distinguished from *C. striata*: smaller (max 30 cm vs 100 cm), more numerous dorsal/anal rays, spotted vs striped pattern
- Distinguished from *C. marulius*: much smaller, different pattern
- Distinguished from *C. gachua*: larger, different coloration

### Biology (FishBase, FAO, IUCN)
- **Physical Description:** Elongated, cylindrical body; large depressed head; large terminal mouth with villiform teeth; single continuous dorsal fin (31-36 rays); long anal fin (22-26 rays); caudal fin rounded; no pelvic fins; scales cycloid; body brownish with numerous dark spots
- **Size:** Max TL: 30 cm; Common: 15-25 cm TL
- **Weight:** Max: 300-500 g; Common: 50-200 g
- **Reproduction:** Oviparous; year-round with peaks in monsoon (Jun-Sep); floating bubble nest; male guards nest; fecundity: 2,000-15,000 eggs/spawn; eggs hatch 24-30 hrs; male guards fry 1-2 weeks

### Ecology (FishBase, FAO, IUCN)
- **Environment:** Freshwater, benthopelagic
- **Habitat:** Shallow, stagnant/slow-flowing waters; dense vegetation; muddy bottom; rice paddies; floodplains; ditches; tolerates very low DO; obligate air-breather
- **Distribution:** South & SE Asia: PK, IN, BD, NP, LK, MM, TH, LA, KH, VN, MY, ID, PH, CN, TW
- **Depth Range:** 0-3 m
- **Diet:** Carnivore/piscivore: fish, crustaceans, insects, amphibians; juveniles: zooplankton, insect larvae
- **Feeding Behaviour:** Ambush predator; lies in wait in vegetation; strikes rapidly; feeds day/night; cannibalistic
- **Adaptations:** Suprabranchial air-breathing organ; mucous secretion for terrestrial movement; parental care; wide environmental tolerances

### Importance (FishBase, FAO, Bangladesh Fisheries)
- **Fisheries:** Major capture fishery (beels, haors, rice fields); high seasonal catches
- **Aquaculture:** Cultured in Bangladesh, India; air-breathing allows high density
- **Cultural:** "Gachua" — common in Bengali cuisine; staple in rural diets
- **Ecological:** Predator in shallow freshwater; controls small fish/invertebrate populations

### Conservation (IUCN Ng et al. 2019)
- **Status:** LC (global)
- **Assessment Date:** 2019
- **Assessor:** Ng, H.H., Britz, R., Chakrabarty, P., Dahanukar, N., Raghavan, R.
- **Population Trend:** Stable (global)
- **Major Threats:** Habitat loss (wetland drainage), pollution, overfishing (localized), pesticide use
- **Conservation Measures:** None species-specific; general wetland protection
- **CITES:** Not listed

### Synonyms (Catalog of Fishes, FishBase, ITIS)
- *Ophiocephalus punctatus* Bloch, 1793 (original)
- *Channa punctata* (Bloch, 1793)
- *Ophiocephalus gachua* Hamilton, 1822
- *Channa gachua* (Hamilton, 1822) — sometimes treated as separate species

## 2. Mystus vittatus — Tengra

### Identity
- **Scientific Name:** Mystus vittatus (Bloch, 1794)
- **Common Name (EN):** Tengra / Striped Mystus
- **Bengali Name:** টেংরা / টেংরা মাছ
- **Authority:** Bloch, 1794 (originally *Silurus vittatus*)

### Taxonomy
| Rank | Name | ID | Status |
|------|------|-----|--------|
| Kingdom | Animalia | animalia | ✓ |
| Phylum | Chordata | chordata | ✓ |
| Class | Actinopterygii | actinopterygii | ✓ |

| Family | Bagridae | bagridae | **Need** |
| Genus | Mystus | mystus | **Need** |
| Species | Mystus vittatus | — | Need species node |

### Key Features (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- Elongated, compressed body; depressed head; 4 pairs of barbels
- Adipose fin present, short; dorsal fin with strong spine + 6-7 rays
- Anal fin 12-15 rays; caudal fin forked
- Body silvery with 3-4 dark longitudinal stripes; ventral pale

### Diagnostic Characteristics (Talwar & Jhingran 1991, FishBase)
- Body depth 4.5-5.5 in SL; head length 3.5-4.0 in SL
- Maxillary barbels reach to pelvic fins; mandibular barbels shorter
- Dorsal fin 1 spine + 6-7 rays; pectoral fin 1 spine + 8-9 rays
- Adipose fin short, posterior to dorsal; caudal fin deeply forked
- 40-45 lateral line scales

### Distinguishing Features (FishBase, FAO)
- Distinguished from *M. gulio*: freshwater vs estuarine; different stripe pattern
- Distinguished from *M. cavasius*: smaller; different barbel length
- Distinguished from *M. tengara*: different coloration; distribution

### Biology (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- **Physical Description:** Elongated, laterally compressed body; depressed head; 4 pairs of barbels (maxillary reach to pelvic fins); adipose fin present; single dorsal fin with strong spine; body silvery with 3-4 dark longitudinal stripes
- **Size:** Max TL: 20-25 cm; Common: 10-15 cm TL
- **Weight:** Max: 50-100 g; Common: 20-50 g
- **Reproduction:** Oviparous; monsoon spawning (Jun-Aug); adhesive eggs; fecundity: 1,000-5,000 eggs/spawn; no parental care reported

### Ecology (FishBase, FAO, IUCN)
- **Environment:** Freshwater, benthopelagic
- **Habitat:** Rivers, streams, beels, haors, ponds, ditches; slow-flowing waters; muddy bottom; vegetation
- **Distribution:** South Asia: PK, IN, BD, NP, LK, MM, TH
- **Depth Range:** 1-10 m
- **Diet:** Omnivore: insects, crustaceans, small fish, detritus, plant matter
- **Feeding Behaviour:** Bottom feeder; forages in mud/vegetation; nocturnal
- **Adaptations:** Air-breathing capable (vascularized gas bladder); tolerates low DO; barbels for sensory detection

### Importance (FishBase, FAO, Bangladesh Fisheries)
- **Fisheries:** Major capture fishery (beels, haors, floodplains); very common in Bangladesh
- **Economic:** Important food fish; high market demand for live fish
- **Cultural:** "Tengra" — staple in Bengali cuisine; traditional recipes
- **Ecological:** Bottom omnivore; insect/crustacean control; prey for larger predators

### Conservation (IUCN Ng 2010)
- **Status:** LC (global)
- **Assessment Date:** 2010
- **Assessor:** Ng, H.H.
- **Population Trend:** Stable
- **Major Threats:** Habitat loss (wetland drainage), pollution, overfishing (localized)
- **Conservation Measures:** None species-specific; general wetland protection
- **CITES:** Not listed

### Synonyms (Catalog of Fishes, FishBase, ITIS)
- *Silurus vittatus* Bloch, 1794 (original)
- *Mystus vittatus* (Bloch, 1794)
- *Macrones vittatus* (Bloch, 1794)
- *Aorichthys vittatus* (Bloch, 1794)

## 3. Labeo calbasu — Kalibaus / Orange-fin Labeo

### Identity
- **Scientific Name:** Labeo calbasu (Hamilton, 1822)
- **Common Name (EN):** Kalibaus / Orange-fin Labeo / Black Rohu
- **Bengali Name:** কালিবাউস / কালো রুই
- **Authority:** Hamilton, 1822 (originally *Cyprinus calbasu*)

### Taxonomy
| Rank | Name | ID | Status |
|------|------|-----|--------|
| Kingdom | Animalia | animalia | ✓ |
| Phylum | Chordata | chordata | ✓ |
| Class | Actinopterygii | actinopterygii | ✓ |
| Order | Cypriniformes | cypriniformes | ✓ |
| Family | Cyprinidae | cyprinidae | ✓ |
| Genus | Labeo | labeo | ✓ |
| Species | Labeo calbasu | — | Need species node |

### Key Features (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- Elongated, fusiform body; inferior mouth; fringed lips; no barbels
- Dorsal fin 11-13 branched rays; anal fin 7-8 rays
- Caudal fin forked; scales cycloid, 40-44 lateral line scales
- Body dark grey to black; fins orange-red; ventral pale

### Diagnostic Characteristics (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- Body depth 3.5-4.0 in SL; head length 3.5-4.0 in SL
- Mouth inferior, fringed lips; no barbels
- Dorsal fin 11-13 branched rays; pharyngeal teeth in 3 rows (5.4.2/2.4.5)
- Scales cycloid, lateral line complete with 40-44 scales

### Distinguishing Features (FishBase, FAO)
- Distinguished from *L. rohita*: darker body color; orange fins vs reddish
- Distinguished from *L. bata*: larger; different lip structure
- Distinguished from *L. gonius*: smaller; different scale counts

### Biology (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- **Physical Description:** Elongated, fusiform body; inferior mouth with fringed lips; no barbels; dorsal fin 11-13 branched rays; caudal fin forked; body dark grey to black; fins orange-red; ventral silvery-white
- **Size:** Max TL: 90 cm; Common: 30-60 cm TL
- **Weight:** Max: 5-8 kg; Common: 1-3 kg
- **Reproduction:** Oviparous; monsoon spawning (Jun-Aug); fecundity: 100,000-500,000 eggs/kg; no parental care; potamodromous migration

### Ecology (FishBase, FAO, IUCN)
- **Environment:** Freshwater, benthopelagic
- **Habitat:** Rivers, lakes, reservoirs, ponds, floodplains; prefers deep pools
- **Distribution:** South Asia: PK, IN, BD, NP, MM
- **Depth Range:** 1-20 m
- **Diet:** Herbivore/detritivore: algae, periphyton, detritus, aquatic plants
- **Feeding Behaviour:** Bottom grazer; grazes on periphyton and algae
- **Adaptations:** Tolerates wide temperature range; bottom feeding adaptation

### Importance (FishBase, FAO, Bangladesh Fisheries)
- **Fisheries:** Important capture fishery; seasonal (monsoon peak)
- **Economic:** High-value food fish in Bangladesh, India; premium price
- **Aquaculture:** Cultured in polyculture with other carps
- **Cultural:** "Kalibaus" — traditional in Bengali cuisine; cultural significance

### Conservation (IUCN Dahanukar 2010)
- **Status:** LC (global)
- **Assessment Date:** 2010
- **Assessor:** Dahanukar, N.
- **Population Trend:** Stable
- **Major Threats:** Overfishing, habitat degradation, pollution, river damming
- **Conservation Measures:** None species-specific; general freshwater habitat protection
- **CITES:** Not listed

### Synonyms (Catalog of Fishes, FishBase, ITIS)
- *Cyprinus calbasu* Hamilton, 1822 (original)
- *Labeo calbasu* (Hamilton, 1822)
- *Rohita calbasu* (Hamilton, 1822)

## 4. Sperata aor — Aor Catfish / Aor

### Identity
- **Scientific Name:** Sperata aor (Hamilton, 1822)
- **Common Name (EN):** Aor Catfish / Aor / Long-whiskered Catfish
- **Bengali Name:** আইড় / আয়র
- **Authority:** Hamilton, 1822 (originally *Silurus aor*)

### Taxonomy
| Rank | Name | ID | Status |
|------|------|-----|--------|
| Kingdom | Animalia | animalia | ✓ |
| Phylum | Chordata | chordata | ✓ |
| Class | Actinopterygii | actinopterygii | ✓ |
| Order | Siluriformes | siluriformes | ✓ |
| Family | Bagridae | bagridae | **Need** |
| Genus | Sperata | sperata | **Need** |
| Species | Sperata aor | — | Need species node |

### Key Features (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- Large predatory catfish; elongated body; very large mouth
- 4 pairs of long barbels (maxillary reach beyond pelvic fins)
- Adipose fin absent; anal fin very long (50-65 rays)
- Caudal fin forked; dorsal fin short, anterior
- Body grey-brown dorsally, lighter ventrally

### Diagnostic Characteristics (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- Head large, depressed; mouth terminal, very wide
- Maxillary barbels extremely long (reach beyond pelvic fins)
- Mandibular barbels long; teeth villiform in bands
- Dorsal fin 1 spine + 5-6 rays; pectoral 1 spine + 10-12 rays
- Anal fin very long (50-65 rays); caudal fin deeply forked
- Scales absent

### Distinguishing Features (FishBase, FAO, Ng & Kottelat 2013)
- Distinguished from *S. seenghala*: shorter maxillary barbels; different distribution
- Distinguished from *Wallago attu*: shorter maxillary barbels; adipose fin absent vs present
- Distinguished from *Ompok* spp.: larger size; no adipose fin

### Biology (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- **Physical Description:** Large predatory catfish; elongated body; very large mouth; 4 pairs of long barbels; adipose fin absent; very long anal fin (50-65 rays); caudal fin forked; body grey-brown dorsally
- **Size:** Max TL: 180 cm; Common: 60-120 cm TL
- **Weight:** Max: 20-30 kg; Common: 5-15 kg
- **Reproduction:** Oviparous; monsoon spawning (Jun-Aug); fecundity: 50,000-100,000 eggs/kg; adhesive eggs in burrows; parental care (male guards nest)

### Ecology (FishBase, FAO, IUCN)
- **Environment:** Freshwater, benthopelagic
- **Habitat:** Deep pools in large rivers; undercut banks; submerged structures; floodplains
- **Distribution:** South Asia: PK, IN, BD, NP, MM
- **Depth Range:** 2-30 m
- **Diet:** Piscivore: fish (carps, catfish, minnows); also crustaceans, molluscs
- **Feeding Behaviour:** Nocturnal ambush predator; uses long barbels; engulfs prey whole
- **Adaptations:** Long barbels for sensory detection; large mouth for engulfing prey; serrated pectoral spines

### Importance (FishBase, FAO, Bangladesh Fisheries)
- **Fisheries:** Important capture fishery; high-value food fish; premium price
- **Economic:** Important in capture fisheries; gear: gillnets, longlines, handlines
- **Cultural:** "Aor" — iconic high-value food fish in Bengali cuisine
- **Ecological:** Top predator in freshwater systems; regulates fish populations

### Conservation (IUCN Ng 2010)
- **Status:** LC (global)
- **Assessment Date:** 2010
- **Assessor:** Ng, H.H.
- **Population Trend:** Unknown
- **Major Threats:** Overfishing, habitat degradation (dams), pollution, water abstraction
- **Conservation Measures:** None species-specific; general freshwater protection
- **CITES:** Not listed

### Synonyms (Catalog of Fishes, FishBase, ITIS)
- *Silurus aor* Hamilton, 1822 (original)
- *Sperata aor* (Hamilton, 1822)
- *Aorichthys aor* (Hamilton, 1822)

## 5. Glossogobius giuris — Bele / Tank Goby

### Identity
- **Scientific Name:** Glossogobius giuris (Hamilton, 1822)
- **Common Name (EN):** Bele / Tank Goby / Bar-eyed Goby
- **Bengali Name:** বেলে / বেলে মাছ
- **Authority:** Hamilton, 1822 (originally *Gobius giuris*)

### Taxonomy
| Rank | Name | ID | Status |
|------|------|-----|--------|
| Kingdom | Animalia | animalia | ✓ |
| Phylum | Chordata | chordata | ✓ |
| Class | Actinopterygii | actinopterygii | ✓ |
| Order | Gobiiformes | gobiiformes | **Need** |
| Family | Gobiidae | gobiidae | **Need** |
| Genus | Glossogobius | glossogobius | **Need** |
| Species | Glossogobius giuris | — | Need species node |

### Key Features (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- Elongated, cylindrical body; depressed head; large eyes
- Two dorsal fins (1st: 6 spines; 2nd: 1 spine + 8-10 rays)
- Anal fin 1 spine + 8-10 rays; pelvic fins fused into disc
- Caudal fin rounded; scales ctenoid; body brownish with dark blotches

### Diagnostic Characteristics (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- Body depth 5.0-6.0 in SL; head length 3.0-3.5 in SL
- Eyes large, close-set, superior; mouth oblique
- 1st dorsal fin VI; 2nd dorsal fin I + 8-10; anal fin I + 8-10
- Pelvic fins fused into adhesive disc; scales ctenoid, 30-35 lateral line scales

### Distinguishing Features (FishBase, FAO)
- Distinguished from *G. celebius*: different distribution; scale counts
- Distinguished from *G. sparsus*: different coloration; scale counts
- Distinguished from other gobies: large eyes; distinctive blotches

### Biology (FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991)
- **Physical Description:** Elongated, cylindrical body; depressed head; large superior eyes; oblique mouth; two dorsal fins; pelvic fins fused into disc; body brownish with dark blotches; ventral pale
- **Size:** Max TL: 30-40 cm; Common: 15-25 cm TL
- **Weight:** Max: 300-500 g; Common: 50-200 g
- **Reproduction:** Oviparous; year-round with peaks in monsoon; adhesive eggs on substrate; male guards nest; fecundity: 1,000-10,000 eggs/spawn

### Ecology (FishBase, FAO, IUCN)
- **Environment:** Freshwater, brackish, marine; benthopelagic
- **Habitat:** Rivers, estuaries, lakes, ponds, rice fields; muddy/sandy bottom; vegetation
- **Distribution:** Indo-Pacific: PK, IN, BD, NP, LK, MM, TH, LA, KH, VN, MY, ID, PH, CN, TW, AU, PG, and wider
- **Depth Range:** 0-10 m
- **Diet:** Carnivore: small fish, crustaceans, insects, worms; opportunistic
- **Feeding Behaviour:** Ambush predator; bottom feeder; uses vision and barbels
- **Adaptations:** Pelvic disc for adhesion; wide salinity tolerance; air-breathing capable

### Importance (FishBase, FAO, Bangladesh Fisheries)
- **Fisheries:** Major capture fishery (beels, haors, rivers, rice fields); ubiquitous in Bangladesh
- **Economic:** Important food fish; high demand; "Bele" — cultural icon
- **Cultural:** "Bele" — staple in Bengali cuisine; traditional recipes; folklore
- **Ecological:** Predator in shallow waters; controls small fish/invertebrates

### Conservation (IUCN Larson 2019)
- **Status:** LC (global)
- **Assessment Date:** 2019
- **Assessor:** Larson, H., Jaafar, Z.
- **Population Trend:** Stable
- **Major Threats:** Habitat loss (wetland drainage), pollution, overfishing (localized)
- **Conservation Measures:** None species-specific; general wetland protection
- **CITES:** Not listed

### Synonyms (Catalog of Fishes, FishBase, ITIS)

- *Glossogobius giuris* (Hamilton, 1822)
