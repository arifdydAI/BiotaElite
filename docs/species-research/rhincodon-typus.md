## 3. Identification

| Aspect | Details | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Key Features** | Largest extant fish; broad, flattened head; terminal mouth; distinct pattern of white spots and stripes on dark dorsal surface; 3 prominent ridges along body; first dorsal fin larger than second; caudal fin crescentic in adults, more heterocercal in juveniles | IUCN, FishBase, FAO |
| **Diagnostic Characteristics** | Body depth ~3.5-4.5 in SL; head broad and flat; mouth terminal, very wide (up to 1.5 m); 300-350 rows of tiny teeth; 5 gill slits; first dorsal fin origin over pelvic fin bases; caudal fin semi-lunate with strong subterminal notch | FishBase, FAO, Compagno 2001 |
| **Distinguishing Features** | Unmistakable: combination of enormous size, terminal mouth, checkerboard spot/stripe pattern, and 3 longitudinal ridges. No other shark species has this combination. | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Similar Species** | None — morphologically unique. Basking shark (Cetorhinus maximus) is large filter-feeder but has different head shape, gill rakers, and no spot pattern. | IUCN, FishBase |

## 4. Biology

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Physical Description** | Massive filter-feeding shark with streamlined body; dorsal surface grey to brown with unique pattern of white spots and vertical/horizontal stripes forming checkerboard; ventral surface white; skin up to 15 cm thick; 3 prominent longitudinal ridges along sides; paired dorsal fins, pectoral fins broad and falcate; pelvic fins moderate; anal fin small; caudal fin semi-lunate in adults. | FishBase, FAO, IUCN |
| **Size** | Max reported: 18-20 m TL (unconfirmed); reliably measured: up to 12-14 m TL; common: 4-12 m TL; size at birth: 40-60 cm TL | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Weight** | Max: up to 34,000 kg (estimated); common: 5,000-15,000 kg | FishBase |
| **Lifespan** | Estimated 70-100+ years; age at maturity: males ~25 years, females ~30+ years; generation length: ~25-30 years | IUCN, FishBase (based on vertebral band counts and growth models) |
| **Anatomy/Morphology** | Cartilaginous skeleton; dermal denticles small, non-overlapping; brain relatively small; olfactory organs highly developed; ampullae of Lorenzini present; filter pads on gill arches modified for plankton filtration | FishBase, Compagno 2001 |
| **Physiology** | Regional endothermy suggested but not confirmed; capable of deep diving (>1,900 m); tolerates wide temperature range | FishBase, IUCN |
| **Behaviour** | Generally solitary or in loose feeding aggregations; highly migratory; surface feeding on plankton blooms; vertical movements diel and seasonal; docile, non-aggressive to humans | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Communication** | No documented vocalization; likely chemical/electrical sensing for prey detection and navigation | FishBase |
| **Reproduction** | Ovoviviparous (aplacental viviparity); only one confirmed pregnant female (Taiwan, 1995) carried ~300 embryos (34-64 cm TL); size at birth 40-60 cm; no confirmed nursery areas; reproductive cycle unknown | IUCN, FishBase, Joung et al. 1996 |
| **Life Cycle** | Neonates rare; juveniles (2-3 m) occasionally coastal; subadults pelagic; adults wide-ranging; no known parental care | IUCN, FishBase |

## 5. Ecology / Habitat

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Environment** | Marine, pelagic, coastal and oceanic; tropical and warm temperate | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Habitat** | Epipelagic to mesopelagic; surface to 1,928 m depth; associated with thermal fronts, upwelling zones, coral reefs, atolls; often near surface when feeding | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Geographic Distribution** | Circumtropical: 30°N to 35°S; all tropical and warm temperate seas except Mediterranean | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Countries** | 100+ coastal states; significant aggregations: Mexico, Belize, Philippines, Maldives, Seychelles, Mozambique, South Africa, Australia, Galápagos, Qatar, UAE | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Depth Range** | 0-1,928 m; mostly 0-100 m when feeding; dives >1,000 m recorded | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Diet** | Planktivore: zooplankton (copepods, euphausiids, fish eggs/larvae), small nekton (anchovies, sardines, mackerel), occasional squid; filter feeds by ram filtration and active suction | IUCN, FishBase, FAO |
| **Feeding Type** | Filter feeder (ram and suction) | FishBase |
| **Feeding Behaviour** | Surface ram filter feeding on dense plankton patches; vertical suction feeding at depth; associates with tuna schools and fish spawning events | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Ecological Role** | Apex filter feeder; nutrient transport between surface and depth; hosts diverse epibionts (copepods, remoras); indicator of productive waters | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Adaptations** | Filter pads on gill arches for efficient plankton capture; thick skin for protection; large liver for buoyancy; regional endothermy potential; deep-diving physiology | FishBase, IUCN |
| **Predators** | Adults: few natural predators (killer whales, large sharks — rare); juveniles: larger sharks, marlins, killer whales | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Prey** | Copepods, euphausiids, fish larvae, small schooling fish, squid | FishBase |
| **Symbiotic Relationships** | Remoras (Echeneidae), copepods (Pandarus rhincodonicus), cleaner fish at aggregation sites | FishBase, IUCN |
