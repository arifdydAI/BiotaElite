## 3. Identification

| Aspect | Details | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Key Features** | Elongated, cylindrical body; large, depressed head; large mouth with sharp teeth; single long dorsal fin (40-50 rays); single long anal fin (30-38 rays); caudal fin rounded; pelvic fins absent; body dark brown/green with irregular blackish bands/stripes; ventral surface pale; prominent dark spot on caudal peduncle (ocellus) in juveniles | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Diagnostic Characteristics** | Body depth 5-6 x SL; head length 2.5-3 x SL; eyes dorsolateral; mouth large, terminal; teeth villiform in bands on jaws, vomer, palatines; dorsal fin origin above pectoral base; anal fin origin below middle of dorsal; scales cycloid, moderate size; lateral line complete, curved; 50-60 lateral line scales | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Distinguishing Features** | Distinguished from *Channa marulius* (Giant Snakehead): smaller max size (<100 cm vs >120 cm), more numerous dorsal/anal rays, different banding pattern (regular stripes vs blotches), no prominent ocellus in adults; from *Channa punctata* (Spotted Snakehead): larger, stripes vs spots, no ocellus in adults; from *Channa gachua* (Dwarf Snakehead): much larger, different coloration | FishBase, FAO |
| **Similar Species** | *Channa marulius* (Giant Snakehead / Gojar) — larger, blotched pattern; *Channa punctata* (Spotted Snakehead / Gachua) — smaller, spotted; *Channa gachua* (Dwarf Snakehead) — small, distinct coloration | FishBase, FAO |

## 4. Biology

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Physical Description** | Elongated, cylindrical, moderately compressed body; large, depressed, snake-like head; large terminal mouth with sharp villiform teeth; eyes dorsolateral; single continuous dorsal fin (40-50 rays); long anal fin (30-38 rays); caudal fin rounded; no pelvic fins; scales cycloid; body dark brown to greenish with distinct blackish vertical bands/stripes; ventral pale; juveniles with prominent ocellus on caudal peduncle | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Size** | Max TL: 100 cm (rarely 120 cm); common: 40-70 cm TL; size at maturity: 25-30 cm TL | FishBase, FAO |
| **Weight** | Max: 3-5 kg; common: 0.5-2 kg | FishBase, FAO |
| **Lifespan** | Estimated 5-8 years in wild; up to 10 years in captivity; no validated age studies | FishBase (no authoritative source) |
| **Anatomy/Morphology** | Suprabranchial chamber (accessory air-breathing organ) well developed; gill rakers short; scales cycloid; lateral line curved; vertebrae 48-51; suprabranchial organ allows aerial respiration | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Physiology** | Obligate air-breather (must surface to breathe); can survive out of water for hours if moist; tolerates very low DO (<0.5 mg/L); tolerates wide pH (6-9); temperature 10-35°C | FishBase, FAO |
| **Behaviour** | Nocturnal/crepuscular; solitary; territorial; ambush predator; hides in vegetation/mud; capable of overland movement during wet conditions; burrows in mud during drought | FishBase, FAO |
| **Communication** | Sound production (grunting) via gas bladder; likely chemical cues | FishBase |
| **Reproduction** | Oviparous; year-round with peaks in monsoon (Jun-Sep) and post-monsoon; nest building in vegetated shallows (both parents); adhesive eggs attached to vegetation; male guards nest; fecundity: 2,000-15,000 eggs per spawn; eggs hatch 24-30 hrs; larvae 4-5 mm | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Life Cycle** | Rapid growth; sexual maturity at 6-12 months; multiple spawns per year; juveniles in marginal vegetation; parental care (male guards nest/fry for 2-3 weeks) | FishBase, FAO |

## 5. Ecology / Habitat

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Environment** | Freshwater, benthopelagic; rivers, lakes, ponds, ditches, rice fields, swamps, beels, haors | FishBase, FAO, IUCN |
| **Habitat** | Shallow, stagnant or slow-flowing waters; dense vegetation; muddy bottom; rice paddies; floodplains; ditches; tolerates very low DO, high turbidity, wide temperature range; obligate air-breather | FishBase, FAO, IUCN |
| **Geographic Distribution** | South and Southeast Asia: Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, China, Taiwan; introduced: Hawaii, Japan, Madagascar, Mauritius, Papua New Guinea, USA (Florida, California) | FishBase, FAO, IUCN, GBIF |
| **Countries** | PK, IN, BD, NP, LK, MM, TH, LA, KH, VN, MY, ID, PH, CN, TW; introduced: US, JP, MG, MU, PG | FishBase, IUCN |
| **Regions (Bangladesh)** | Ubiquitous: rivers, beels, haors, floodplains, ponds, ditches, rice fields; Sundarbans freshwater zones | Project legacy, Bangladesh Fisheries Statistical Yearbook |
| **Depth Range** | 0-5 m; surface to shallow bottom | FishBase, FAO |
| **Diet** | Carnivore/piscivore: fish (carps, minnows, own young), crustaceans (prawns, crabs), insects, amphibians, molluscs; juveniles: zooplankton, insect larvae | FishBase, FAO |
| **Feeding Type** | Carnivore / Piscivore | FishBase |
| **Feeding Behaviour** | Ambush predator; lies in wait in vegetation; strikes rapidly; feeds day and night; cannibalistic (especially juveniles) | FishBase, FAO |
| **Ecological Role** | Predator in shallow freshwater; controls small fish/invertebrate populations; important in rice-field ecosystems; indicator of wetland health | FishBase, FAO |
| **Adaptations** | Suprabranchial air-breathing organ (allows survival in hypoxic water); mucous secretion for terrestrial movement; parental care; wide environmental tolerances | FishBase, FAO |
| **Predators** | Adults: humans, large fish, birds, otters; juveniles: larger fish, birds, snakes, other snakeheads | FishBase, FAO |
| **Prey** | Fish (carps, minnows, snakeheads), crustaceans, insects, amphibians, molluscs | FishBase, FAO |
| **Symbiotic Relationships** | None documented | — |

## 6. Importance

| Aspect | Assessment | Source |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **Ecological** | Key predator in shallow freshwater; controls prey populations; rice-field ecosystem component | FishBase, FAO |
| **Economic** | Important food fish in Bangladesh, India, SE Asia; high market demand; premium price for live fish; significant in capture and culture fisheries | FishBase, FAO, Bangladesh Fisheries Statistical Yearbook |
