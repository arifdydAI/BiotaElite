**Taxonomic Note**: Family Heteropneustidae is monotypic (or contains 2 species: *H. fossilis* and *H. microps* from Sri Lanka). Some older classifications place in Siluridae or Saccobranchidae. Current consensus: Heteropneustidae.

## 3. Identification

| Aspect | Details | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Key Features** | Elongated, cylindrical body; compressed posteriorly; large, depressed head; 4 pairs of barbels (1 maxillary, 1 nasal, 2 mandibular); very long air sacs (suprabranchial) extending along body cavity to near tail; dorsal fin short, far anterior (1 spine + 6 rays); adipose fin absent; anal fin very long (60-80 rays); caudal fin rounded; pectoral fin with strong, serrated spine (venomous); pelvic fin 6 rays; body grey-brown to black, lighter ventrally | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Diagnostic Characteristics** | Body depth 6-7 x SL; head length 3.5-4 x SL; mouth terminal; teeth villiform in bands on jaws and palate; nasal barbels reach to eyes; maxillary barbels reach to pectoral fins; mandibular barbels shorter; air sacs paired, long, extending from gill chamber to near caudal fin; dorsal fin 1 spine + 6 rays; anal fin 60-80 rays; pectoral spine strong, serrated, venomous; scales absent | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Distinguishing Features** | Unique among Asian catfishes: extremely long air sacs extending along body cavity; 4 pairs of barbels (nasal pair present); venomous pectoral spine; no adipose fin; very long anal fin; distinguished from *Clarias* (African air-breathing catfish) by nasal barbels, air sac morphology, venomous spine; from *Ompok* by air sacs, venomous spine, no adipose fin | FishBase, FAO, Ng & Kottelat 2013 |
| **Similar Species** | *Heteropneustes microps* (Sri Lanka) — smaller, smaller eyes, possible synonym; *Clarias gariepinus* (African Sharptooth Catfish) — introduced, different air-breathing organ, no nasal barbels, no venomous spine; *Ompok* spp. — adipose fin present, no air sacs, no venomous spine | FishBase, FAO, Ng & Kottelat 2013 |

## 4. Biology

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Physical Description** | Elongated, cylindrical anteriorly, compressed posteriorly; large, depressed head; terminal mouth; 4 pairs of barbels; most distinctive: paired, elongated air sacs (suprabranchial diverticula) extending from gill chamber along body cavity almost to caudal fin; short dorsal fin (1 spine + 6 rays) far forward; no adipose fin; very long anal fin (60-80 rays); caudal fin rounded; strong, serrated pectoral spine (venomous); skin naked; coloration dark grey to black dorsally, lighter ventrally | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Size** | Max TL: 30-40 cm (rarely 50 cm); common: 15-25 cm TL; size at maturity: 10-15 cm TL | FishBase, FAO |
| **Weight** | Max: 300-500 g; common: 50-200 g | FishBase, FAO |
| **Lifespan** | Estimated 5-8 years; no validated age studies | FishBase (no authoritative source) |
| **Anatomy/Morphology** | Paired, elongated suprabranchial air sacs (diverticula of gill chamber) extending along body cavity to near caudal fin; highly vascularized for gas exchange; 4 pairs of barbels (maxillary, nasal, 2 mandibular); villiform teeth in bands; strong, serrated pectoral spine with venom gland; naked skin; no adipose fin; vertebrae 40-45 | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991, Singh & Singh 2015 |
| **Physiology** | Obligate air-breather (must surface regularly); air sacs function as accessory respiratory organs; highly vascularized epithelium; can survive out of water for hours if moist; pectoral spine venom causes intense pain, inflammation; tolerates very low DO, high ammonia, wide pH/temperature | FishBase, FAO, Singh & Singh 2015 |
| **Behaviour** | Nocturnal; solitary; bottom-dweller; hides in mud, vegetation, burrows; territorial; capable of overland movement during wet conditions; burrows in mud during drought; aestivates in dry season | FishBase, FAO |
| **Communication** | Sound production (grunting) via gas bladder/air sacs; likely chemical cues | FishBase |
| **Reproduction** | Oviparous; monsoon spawning (Jun-Aug); adhesive eggs deposited in burrows/nests in muddy banks; parental care reported (both parents guard); fecundity: 2,000-10,000 eggs per spawn; eggs large (2-3 mm); hatch 24-36 hrs; male guards nest | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Life Cycle** | Rapid growth; sexual maturity at 6-12 months; multiple spawns per year (monsoon); juveniles in shallow margins; parental care (nest guarding) | FishBase, FAO |
