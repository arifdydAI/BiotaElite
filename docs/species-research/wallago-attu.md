## 3. Identification

| Aspect | Details | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Key Features** | Large predatory catfish; elongated, cylindrical body; very large mouth extending beyond eyes; 2 pairs of long barbels (maxillary reaching pectoral fins, mandibular shorter); small eyes; dorsal fin short, anterior; adipose fin absent; anal fin long (60-80 rays); caudal fin forked; body grey to brown dorsally, lighter ventrally | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Diagnostic Characteristics** | Head large, depressed; mouth terminal, very wide; maxillary barbels extremely long (reach to pelvic fins or beyond); mandibular barbels long; teeth villiform in bands on jaws and vomer; dorsal fin with 1 spine + 4-5 rays; pectoral fin with 1 spine + 11-14 rays; pelvic fin 6 rays; anal fin very long (60-80 rays); caudal fin deeply forked; scales absent | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Distinguishing Features** | Distinguished from *Wallago leerii* (Helicopter Catfish) by: longer maxillary barbels (reach pelvic fins vs pectoral fins), more anal fin rays (60-80 vs 50-60), different distribution (Indian subcontinent vs SE Asia); from *Ompok* spp. by larger size, longer barbels, anal fin origin anterior to dorsal fin | FishBase, FAO, Ng & Kottelat 2013 |
| **Similar Species** | *Wallago leerii* (Helicopter Catfish) — SE Asia, shorter maxillary barbels; *Ompok bimaculatus* (Butter Catfish) — smaller, adipose fin present; *Silurus* spp. — adipose fin present | FishBase, FAO |

## 4. Biology

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Physical Description** | Large, predatory catfish with elongated cylindrical body; head large, depressed; mouth enormous, terminal, extending well behind eyes; 2 pairs of very long barbels (maxillary reach to or beyond pelvic fins); eyes small, dorsolateral; skin naked (no scales); single short dorsal fin far anterior; no adipose fin; very long anal fin (60-80 rays) running most of ventral length; caudal fin deeply forked; coloration grey-brown dorsally, silvery-white ventrally | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Size** | Max TL: 200-250 cm (reports up to 250 cm); common: 100-150 cm TL; size at maturity: ~60-80 cm TL | FishBase, IUCN, FAO |
| **Weight** | Max: 40-60 kg (reports up to 60-80 kg); common: 5-20 kg | FishBase, FAO |
| **Lifespan** | Unknown; estimated 15-20+ years based on size; no validated age studies | FishBase (no authoritative source) |
| **Anatomy/Morphology** | Naked skin; Weberian apparatus; large gas bladder (2 chambers); villiform teeth in bands; Weberian ossicles; large pectoral spines with serrations | FishBase, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Physiology** | Air-breathing capable (vascularized gas bladder); tolerates low oxygen; can survive out of water for extended periods | FishBase, FAO |
| **Behaviour** | Nocturnal, solitary, highly predatory; ambush predator; hides in deep pools, undercut banks, submerged structures; territorial; cannibalistic; capable of breathing air | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Communication** | Likely chemical/pheromonal; sound production (stridulation) possible via pectoral spines | FishBase |
| **Reproduction** | Oviparous; spawning monsoon (June-August); adhesive eggs deposited in nests/cavities; parental care reported (male guards nest); fecundity: 10,000-50,000 eggs/kg; eggs large (3-4 mm) | FishBase, FAO, Talwar & Jhingran 1991 |
| **Life Cycle** | Eggs hatch 24-36 hours; larvae 5-6 mm; yolk absorbed in 3-4 days; rapid growth; sexual maturity at 2-3 years; juveniles in shallow margins | FishBase, FAO |
