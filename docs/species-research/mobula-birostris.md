**Synonyms** (Catalog of Fishes, FishBase, ITIS, IUCN):
- *Raja birostris* Walbaum, 1792 (original)
- *Manta birostris* (Walbaum, 1792) — widely used until 2017
- *Manta hamiltoni* Springer & Burgess, 1963
- *Manta raya* (Müller & Henle, 1841)
- *Cephaloptera birostris* (Walbaum, 1792)
- *Cerapterus birostris* (Walbaum, 1792)
- *Brachioptilon birostris* (Walbaum, 1792)
- *Diabolus birostris* (Walbaum, 1792)

**Taxonomic Note**: Genus *Manta* synonymized with *Mobula* by White et al. (2017) based on molecular phylogeny. *Manta birostris* is now *Mobula birostris*. Some older sources still use *Manta*.

## 3. Identification

| Aspect | Details | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Key Features** | Largest ray species; disc width up to 7-9 m; distinctively broad, diamond-shaped disc; terminal mouth; cephalic fins prominent, rolled when swimming, unfurled when feeding; dorsal surface black to dark brown with white shoulder patches; ventral surface white with dark spots/markings between gill slits; long, whip-like tail (usually without spine in adults) | IUCN, FishBase, FAO, White et al. 2017 |
| **Diagnostic Characteristics** | Disc width 2.2-2.4 x disc length; mouth terminal, wide; 18-20 cephalic fin lobes; teeth in upper jaw only (300+ rows, small, peg-like); gill slits 5 pairs, ventral; dorsal fin small, at tail base; caudal spine usually absent in adults (present in juveniles); skin rough with denticles | FishBase, FAO, White et al. 2017 |
| **Distinguishing Features** | Distinguished from *Mobula alfredi* (Reef Manta): larger size (DW >5 m vs <5 m), more oceanic distribution, T-shaped white shoulder patches (vs Y-shaped), caudal spine absent in adults (vs present), denticles cover entire disc (vs posterior only), mouth darker (vs white) | IUCN, FishBase, Marshall et al. 2009, White et al. 2017 |
| **Similar Species** | *Mobula alfredi* (Reef Manta Ray) — smaller, coastal, Y-shaped shoulder patches, caudal spine present; *Mobula tarapacana* (Sicklefin Devil Ray) — smaller, different coloration, pointed wingtips | IUCN, FishBase |

## 4. Biology

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Physical Description** | Massive pelagic ray with rhomboidal disc; terminal mouth flanked by large cephalic fins used to funnel plankton; dorsal surface uniformly dark with distinctive white/cream shoulder markings forming T-shape; ventral white with dark blotches between gill slits (unique to individuals); tail slender, whip-like, usually lacking spine in adults; small dorsal fin at tail base | FishBase, FAO, IUCN, Marshall et al. 2009 |
| **Size** | Max disc width: 7-9 m (reliably 7 m); common: 4-7 m DW; size at birth: 1.2-1.5 m DW | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Weight** | Max: ~2,000-3,000 kg (estimated); common: 500-1,500 kg | FishBase, IUCN |
| **Lifespan** | Estimated 40-50+ years; generation length: ~20 years; age at maturity: males ~8-10 years, females ~10-15 years | IUCN, FishBase (based on growth bands in vertebrae) |
| **Anatomy/Morphology** | Cartilaginous skeleton; filter plates on gill arches; cephalic fins highly vascularized; skin covered in dermal denticles; no caudal spine in adults; single dorsal fin | FishBase, White et al. 2017 |
| **Physiology** | Capable of deep diving (>1,000 m); regional endothermy in brain/eyes suspected; large pectoral fins for efficient lift-based swimming | FishBase, IUCN |
| **Behaviour** | Pelagic, highly migratory; solitary or in feeding/mating aggregations; visits cleaning stations; breaching observed; site fidelity at aggregation areas; capable of transoceanic movements | IUCN, FishBase, Marshall et al. 2011 |
| **Communication** | No vocalization; likely electroreception (ampullae of Lorenzini) for prey detection and navigation; visual cues at cleaning stations | FishBase |
| **Reproduction** | Ovoviviparous (aplacental viviparity); single pup per litter (rarely 2); gestation ~12-13 months; size at birth 1.2-1.5 m DW; no parental care; reproductive cycle ~2-5 years; mating observed at aggregation sites | IUCN, FishBase, Marshall et al. 2011 |
| **Life Cycle** | Neonates independent at birth; juveniles coastal/shelf; subadults range widely; adults pelagic; no nursery areas confirmed | IUCN, FishBase |

## 5. Ecology / Habitat

| Field | Value | Source |
|-------|-------|--------|
| **Environment** | Marine, pelagic, oceanic and coastal; tropical, subtropical, temperate | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Habitat** | Surface to 1,000+ m; epipelagic to mesopelagic; open ocean, continental shelves, seamounts, island chains; visits cleaning stations on reefs; often near surface when feeding | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Geographic Distribution** | Circumglobal in tropical to temperate waters: ~30°N to 30°S; coastal and oceanic; seasonal migrations to higher latitudes in summer | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Countries** | 80+ coastal states; major aggregations: Maldives, Indonesia, Philippines, Mexico, Ecuador, Mozambique, South Africa, Australia, Hawaii, Yemen, Sri Lanka | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Depth Range** | 0-1,000+ m; commonly 0-200 m; deep dives >1,000 m recorded via satellite tags | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Diet** | Planktivore: zooplankton (copepods, euphausiids, mysids), fish larvae, small crustaceans; filter feeds via gill rakers | IUCN, FishBase, FAO |
| **Feeding Type** | Filter feeder (ram filtration) | FishBase |
| **Feeding Behaviour** | Ram filter feeding on dense plankton patches; barrel rolls and somersaults to concentrate prey; unfurls cephalic fins to funnel water; feeds at surface and depth; associates with upwelling, fronts, spawning events | IUCN, FishBase, Marshall et al. 2011 |
| **Ecological Role** | Apex filter feeder; nutrient transport (surface to depth); hosts epibionts (copepods, remoras); supports cleaning station ecosystems; indicator of productive waters | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Adaptations** | Cephalic fins for prey funneling; efficient lift-based swimming; deep-diving physiology; regional endothermy in brain/eyes; counter-current heat exchange | FishBase, IUCN |
| **Predators** | Adults: few (killer whales, large sharks — rare); juveniles: large sharks, killer whales | IUCN, FishBase |
| **Prey** | Copepods, euphausiids, mysids, fish larvae, small crustaceans | FishBase |
| **Symbiotic Relationships** | Remoras (Echeneidae), copepods, cleaner fish (Labroides spp., Thalassoma spp.) at cleaning stations | FishBase, IUCN |
