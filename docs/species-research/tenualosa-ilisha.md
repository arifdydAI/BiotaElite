## 3. Identification

**Key Features** (FishBase, IUCN, Marine Biodiversity BD):
- Body compressed, fusiform
- Dorsal fin with 18-21 rays (D1 3+15; D2 absent)
- Anal fin with 20-22 rays (A 2+18)
- Pectoral fin 15 rays (P1 1+14)
- Pelvic fin 8 rays (P2 1+7)
- Back blue-green, flanks silvery
- Series of black blotches along flanks (may disappear in large adults)
- No dorsal fin spine
- Belly with distinct keel of scutes (30-35)
- Gill rakers fine, numerous (100-250)

**Diagnostic Characteristics** (IUCN, FishBase, FAO):
- Body depth 3.0-3.5 times in standard length
- Head length 3.5-4.0 times in standard length
- Eye diameter 4.0-5.0 times in head length
- Mouth terminal, upper jaw not reaching eye
- Gill rakers very long and numerous (100-250)
- Belly keel with 30-35 scutes
- Dorsal fin inserted ahead of pelvic fins
- No dorsal fin spine
- Anal fin long (20-22 rays)

**Distinguishing Features** (IUCN, FishBase):
- Distinguished from other Tenualosa species by: number of gill rakers, anal fin ray count, blotch pattern
- T. ilisha: 100-250 gill rakers, 20-22 anal rays
- T. toli: fewer gill rakers, different distribution

**Source**: IUCN Red List, FishBase, FAO, Marine Biodiversity BD, ITIS

## 5. Biology

**Habitat** (IUCN, FishBase, FAO):
- Marine, freshwater, brackish (anadromous)
- Pelagic, coastal waters, estuaries, rivers
- Marine: coastal waters, continental shelf
- Freshwater: rivers up to 1200 km upstream
- Temperature: 23-29°C (spawning)

**Behavior** (IUCN, FishBase):
- Anadromous migration
- Forms schools
- Pelagic, coastal waters
- Adults migrate from sea up river to spawn
- Juveniles descend to estuary/sea
- Social: Solitary or loose aggregations at feeding sites

**Reproduction** (IUCN, FishBase, FAO):
- Sexual maturity: 1-2 years
- Spawning season: Year-round with peaks in monsoon and post-monsoon
- Spawning temperature: 23-29°C
- Fecundity: 1.5-2.5 million eggs per female
- Spawning substrate: Freshwater river reaches with sandy/muddy bottom
- No parental care
- Migration: Anadromous (sea to freshwater to spawn; juveniles descend to estuary/sea)
- Migration distance: Up to 1,200 km upstream in Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna system

**Diet** (IUCN, FishBase, FAO):
- Feeding type: Planktivore
- Feeds on: Phytoplankton, zooplankton, small crustaceans
- Filter feeds using gill rakers
- Pelagic filter feeder

**Source Agreement**: IUCN, FishBase, FAO agree on biological parameters

## 10. Ecology

**Ecological Role** (IUCN, FishBase, FAO):
- Important planktivore in coastal and riverine ecosystems
- Nutrient cycling between marine and freshwater
- Prey for larger piscivorous fish, dolphins, birds
- Important in estuarine and riverine food webs

**Adaptations** (IUCN, FishBase):
- Euryhaline (tolerates wide salinity range)
- Anadromous life cycle
- Long-distance migration capability
- High fecundity

**Predators** (IUCN, FishBase): Larger piscivorous fish, dolphins, birds

**Threats** (IUCN):
- Overfishing
- Dams and barrages (Farakka barrage) blocking migration
- Industrial pollution
- Habitat degradation

**Source Agreement**: IUCN, FishBase, FAO agree on ecological role and threats
